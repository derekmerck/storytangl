# Salon Season

**Status:** design brief, revised 2026-10-15. Nothing is built.

- All three engine checkpoints are approved (Appendix A).
- Checkpoint 2 was revised on approval: the coda *assembles* cases instead of
  inspecting them.
- Grafting this world onto *Coronate the Regent* is deferred.

## 1. The idea

**A dating sim whose favor meters are reread by a revolution.**

| Phase | Setting | What you are doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Season** | Paris, spring 1789 | Dressing, flirting, gossiping, collecting favor. You think you are climbing. You are actually choosing who will carry you onto the Committee, and how much influence you will have once you get there |
| **The Terror** | 1793–94 | Your proxy sits on the Committee. The people you knew in 1789 come before it. You *assemble* their cases, from the truth plus whatever gossip and favor you saved, to get the outcome you want: free a friend, finish a rival, settle a debt |

The record never changes. Only the regime reading it changes. Thermidor reads
it a third time, and that time the case is yours.

What drives the design is mechanical as much as narrative:

- take favor, standing and gossip, the formalism of a dating sim;
- reinterpret them dramatically under a new regime;
- play out the consequences on people the player actually cares about.

### Lineage and IP

The mechanical lineage is *Ambition: A Minuet in Power* (Joy Manufacturing
Co.): faction favor gates events, you grind at salons, you court at rendezvous.
Its *shapes* carry over: the vanished patron, the salon rival, the gossip
broker, the debts, a trial. **No source prose, names, characters or plot are
used.** History supplies the background: places, figures, the Law of Suspects,
the *certificat de civisme*.

## 2. The Season: laying groundwork

### What you are really choosing

- **Your proxy.** The suitor you bind yourself to (marriage, alliance,
  patronage) becomes your seat on the Committee in 1793.
  - The proxy does **not** have to come from your preferred faction. A
    calculating player courts the revolutionary while dressing for the Crown.
  - Courtship is a decision about future power, made with dating-sim tools.
- **Your influence.** How much weight you will be able to push through your
  proxy. It is read off Season favor (Appendix A.1).
- **Your exposure.** Everything that raised you in 1789 can be read against
  you in 1793: Crown esteem, things you said, debts, and the papers you are
  living on.

### The cast: tropes with event lines that carry through

The people who matter get **little event lines in the Season that pay off as
cases in the Terror.** No unmemorable NPCs met once at a party.

| Trope | Season line | Terror payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The missing lover** | The patron who set you up in Paris, then vanished. His letters and invitations still arrive. You are, in a sense, living on his name | He resurfaces: an émigré, a spy, or a prisoner. You prepare his defense, or his end |
| **The rival** | Salon adversary. Snubs, a masquerade, a public humiliation that you deal or receive | She comes before the Committee. You can sandbag her case and let it kill her |
| **The spy** | Gossip broker. Trades rumors, sells to everyone, and knows things about you | A source of evidence in both directions. The one person who can expose your own papers |
| **The modiste** | Dresses you for the season. Kind, generous with credit. You owe her a great deal | Accused of hoarding cloth, or of dressing aristocrats. Saving her costs influence. Letting her fall erases the debt |
| **The landlord** | Collects rent. Unpleasant, and holds your debts | The same temptation without the kindness: one condemnation and the debt is gone |
| **The suitors** | Three, one per faction. One becomes your proxy | See below |

**Debts make the Terror personal.** The two people you owe the most are the two
whose deaths would free you. Whether you defend the kind one, and whether you
can resist using the Tribunal as debt relief against the cruel one, is the kind
of choice a dating sim never asks you to make.

### The suitors after 1789

Whoever is *not* your proxy is still in the story, or has been killed:

- **The revolutionary leader** may already have fallen, purged by the faction
  that came after his.
- **The prince**, a royal-blood suitor, may have turned champion of the
  Revolution and now sits on the Committee beside you. The historical
  precedent is Philippe Égalité: a royal duke who voted for the King's death
  and was guillotined anyway.
- **The bourgeois heir** may be a profiteer, a Girondin, or a corpse.

### Women on the Committee

There is a direct path, but it is narrow:

1. Your proxy is condemned. If you married the prince, his blood finally
   catches up with him.
2. You survive his fall.
3. You escape his shadow. That shadow is a case you must assemble for
   yourself.

Then you take the seat in your own name. It is earned, rare, and it costs
someone you chose.

### Season loop (brief)

- **Calendar.** About three weeks. Afternoons are for rendezvous, shopping or
  the rumor mill. Evenings are salons, reached by invitation letters
  (Appendix A.3).
- **Factions.** Crown and Revolution are the majors and are opposed. The
  Bourgeoisie is the minor and swings.
- **Salons.** A few activity slots: a parlour game, a gossip dilemma, a snoop,
  a cast beat. Winning favor and gossip there opens grander salons.
- **Outfits.** An outfit is an *assembly* judged against the host faction's
  taste. Remember this operation: the Terror uses it again on cases.
- **Gossip dilemmas.** Questions like "Is the Queen's necklace power, or
  excess?" Each one moves favor now, and each answer goes **on the record**.

## 3. The Terror: assembling cases

### Regime change rereads the record

Nothing is rewritten. The same favor is read through new weights:

- Revolution standing becomes *influence*.
- Crown standing becomes *taint*.
- Renown cuts both ways: the prominent have more power and more exposure.

The political map also redraws. The Revolution splits into Montagnards,
Girondins, Hébertists and Indulgents. Your 1789 revolutionary friends land on
different sides, so the faction coupling runs again over new factions.

### The core mechanic: assemble the packet

This is credentials, inverted. In the checkpoint games, you *check* a packet
someone else assembled. Here **you assemble it**, then the Tribunal checks it,
and the test is whether it produces **the outcome you wanted**.

| Packet part | What it is in the Terror |
| --- | --- |
| **Accusations** | The accused's deeds and associations. Some are true, drawn from the Season. Some the prosecution already holds |
| **Testimony and certificates** | The documents that answer an accusation: a *certificat de civisme*, a patriot's testimony, an alibi |
| **The rules** | The Law of Suspects: which deeds are crimes for which estate, and what answers them |
| **Your pool** | True evidence the accused actually has, plus the gossip tokens you squirrelled away, plus forgeries you can commission |
| **Your budget** | Influence. Each piece you present costs some. Forgeries cost more |
| **Your goal** | Declared at the start: acquit, exile or condemn |

Your moves mirror the checker's sins:

| Checker's sin (credentials) | Your move (Terror) | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ignoring an error | **Sandbag** | Withhold the exculpatory piece you hold, and let a rival hang |
| Inventing an error | **Embellish** | Add a forged testimony to free a friend, or a forged accusation to bury an enemy |
| (none) | **Challenge** | Spend influence to strike a prosecution piece |

Forged pieces carry a risk of being caught. Being caught raises **suspicion**,
which is the Terror's own meter, pointed at you.

### The cases that matter

Every docket is drawn from the cast:

- your ex-lover;
- your rival;
- the spy;
- your modiste;
- your landlord;
- a suitor who is not your proxy;
- occasionally your proxy's enemy, whom your proxy wants dead.

Each case is a small dilemma with history behind it. The influence you spend on one
case is influence you don't have for the next.

### Thermidor: the third reading

The regime turns again. The Revolution's favorites become its taint. Your own
case comes up, assembled from **your journal**:

- what you said at salons;
- whom you condemned;
- whom you saved;
- the papers you have been living on since the missing lover arranged them.

You assemble your own defense from whatever influence and goodwill remain.

**Endings, read off the shape of your record:**

| Ending | Record | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Robespierre** | All-in on one corner | You rose, then fell at Thermidor |
| **Talleyrand** | Hedged toward the center | You survive every regime, and no one loves you |
| **Émigré** | You got out | Fled, possibly with whoever you saved |
| **Martyr** | Influence spent on others | They lived, and the cost was you |

**Special endings: clearing the board.** These are rarer, earned by who you
eliminated rather than by the shape of your record:

- **The Incorruptible** (the title is still to be settled; Robespierre's own
  epithet is the ironic candidate).
  - *Condition:* the missing lover and all three suitors are gone, and you sit
    on the Committee in your own name.
  - *Outcome:* there is no one left to fall with or through. Everyone who
    remains is afraid of you, and they proclaim you the Revolution's
    uncontested voice. This is the only way to escape the Robespierre fate
    *from* the top: by being the last one standing, not by hedging.
- **The Restoration gambit.**
  - *Condition:* everyone is gone except the prince.
  - *Outcome:* a foreign crown backs a counter-revolution with him as its
    figurehead and you as its architect. Then the restored order does to you
    what Thermidor did to Robespierre: a regicide's accomplice is the first
    liability a new king removes.
  - *Survival* requires threading a further needle. That needle is still to be
    designed. Candidates include binding the prince to you so completely that
    removing you would cost him his crown, or holding something over the
    foreign backers.

Both are tests of the cast-as-cases design. The people you would have to
eliminate are the same people the Season made you care about, so the board
only clears if the player is willing to go through every one of them.

## 4. Why this is more interesting than the source

The source never contests its frame. Salons are grind, rendezvous are an
interchangeable cross-product, favor buys only more favor, and its mystery runs
beside the social loop rather than through it.

| What changes | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **The genre is on trial** | Charm, appearance and patronage are aristocratic sociability. A dating sim set in 1789 can be about its own values |
| **Rereading, not rewriting** | The engine choice that regime change writes nothing (Appendix A.1) is also the theme. History rereads people. Every trivial choice gains weight retroactively |
| **Dramatic irony** | The player knows 1793 is coming and the character doesn't. **Advertise the coda instead of hiding it.** Every flirtation becomes a visible future liability, and a second playthrough is a different game |
| **People, not abstractions** | The Terror's cases are people you dressed with, owe money to, slept with, and humiliated |

## 5. What it drives in the engine

### Integration demo: existing mechanics composed

| Mechanic | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| Assembly (`ComponentManager`, slots, budgets) | Outfits in the Season, case packets in the Terror. The same operation under two regimes |
| Credentials kernel | `derive_disposition`, `Restrictions`, packet materialization |
| Asset wallet and tokens | Favor, livres, gossip tokens, invitation letters |
| Progression | Parlour-game stat challenges with `forced_outcome` |
| Sandbox schedule | The calendar |
| Provisioning | Invitations as open edges that materialize salons on traversal |

### New mechanics it forces

1. **Readouts over favor** (approved, Appendix A.1). Standing, renown and
   alignment are computed from favor; regime change is new readout weights.
2. **Assembly-mode credentials** (checkpoint 2, revised, Appendix A.2). The
   player builds the packet toward a goal, and the evaluator judges whether it
   gets there.
3. **The journal as evidence.** The Thermidor case is assembled by querying the
   player's own journal: statements, verdicts, associations. This is the first
   real consumer for journal retrieval (#191: journal → slice → selector). It
   needs its own design pass before the coda's last act. It is not needed for
   the first slice.

## 6. First slice: thin both ways

The Season alone is the source's boring part, and the Terror is where the idea
lives. So build a vertical slice:

- **One Season week:**
  - two salons
  - the rival line and the modiste line
  - two suitors
  - one gossip dilemma
- **The boundary:** a time skip that rereads the record.
- **One Committee session:** two cases, the rival and the modiste, assembled
  toward a declared goal.

The slice tests the only question that matters first: **does the flip land?**
Everything else (the full cast, the Revolution splitting, Thermidor, the
journal as evidence, endings) comes after that answer.

## 7. Open questions

- **How much Season content?** Enough for the flip to hurt, and no more. The
  slice will calibrate this.
- **The protagonist's papers.** How explicit is the forged identity, and when
  does the player learn about it?
- **The proxy's agency.** Does your proxy always vote your way, or does
  low-influence leave them to follow their own faction?
- **Historical figures.** Robespierre, Danton and others in the background
  only, or as recurring presences?

## Appendix A. Engine resolutions

### A.1 Checkpoint 1: favor and readouts (approved)

- **Store.** Raw favor mass per faction, held in the player's `AssetWallet`.
  `Faction` subclasses `CountableAsset`. Mass is nonnegative and clamped at
  zero.
- **Readouts,** computed and never stored:
  - *renown* is the total mass, using the `total_value` path;
  - *alignment* is mass divided by renown, a barycentric point;
  - *standing(f)* is `Quality(w[f] · favor)`, with each faction authoring its
    weight row `w[f]`.
- **Coupling is the weights.** For example, the Crown's row is
  `{crown: 1, revolution: -0.5}`.
- **One pathway.** The wallet gains a weighted-sum method, and `total_value`
  is re-expressed through it.
- **Regime change** adds readouts over the same store (influence, taint). The phase
  boundary writes nothing.

### A.2 Checkpoint 2: assembly-mode credentials (approved)

This supersedes the earlier inspection-mode resolution. That design (seeded
findings, `present_evidence`, a `case_mutations` overlay) is no longer needed.

**Truth is the offer.** The world builds one `ScenarioOffer` per case from
Season state:

- estate → `region`
- deeds → `contraband`
- missing papers → `failure_modes`
- actor uid → `bearer_id`

Re-materializing the offer always gives the truth back, so no overlay is
needed to protect it.

**The packet is the record.** The case's `CredentialPacketManager` is what the
player assembles:

- Prosecution pieces are pre-assigned.
- The player's pool sits in the existing `unpresented` slot. That pool is the
  true evidence the accused has, plus the player's gossip tokens converted to
  components, plus forgeries.
- **Present** = `assign` to the packet slot.
- **Sandbag** = leave a piece unpresented.
- **Challenge** = `unassign` a prosecution piece, at a influence cost.
- **Embellish** = commission a forged component, then present it.
- Influence is a `tracked_resource` on the manager. Every component has a cost,
  and `assign` already refuses what the budget can't afford.

**Testimony answers accusations the way permits answer contraband.** A deed is
covered by a valid testimony keyed to its indication. A forged testimony is a
`FORGED` component. The rules table is the Law of Suspects.

**Commit and evaluate.** The player declares a goal (acquit, exile or condemn)
and commits.

- The evaluator runs `derive_disposition` on the assembled packet.
- Each forged component makes one catch roll, seeded and replayable.
- **Uncaught:** the verdict is the record's derivation.
- **Caught:** the verdict is derived on the truth (the forgery removed and the
  sandbagged pieces restored), and suspicion rises.
- Success means the verdict matches the goal.
- The result records the goal, both derivations, `caught`, and the pieces
  played.

**Kernel footprint.** A sibling game mode over the same kernel: assembly moves
and a goal-match result, reusing `derive_disposition`, the packet manager,
`materialize` and the assembly budget. Inspection mode (`credential_gate`,
`hall_monitor`) is untouched. Disposition still ≠ fate: the world decides what
a verdict means for each person.

### A.3 Checkpoint 3: no generic offer (approved)

An invitation is an **invitation-letter asset token** whose locals hold its
sampled parameters. Those parameters are sampled once, at UPDATE. The evening
hub offers one choice per letter. Each choice's successor requirement carries
the salon template as `fallback_templ`, and provisioning materializes the salon
on traversal.

`ScenarioOffer` stays credentials' domain record. Promoting it would duplicate
`EntityTemplate` and provisioning.

## Appendix C. Slice validation

- **Flip.** The same Season record yields different influence and taint under the
  Terror readouts. The boundary writes nothing.
- **Assembly.** For a fixed case, presenting the exculpatory piece acquits, and
  sandbagging it condemns.
- **Assembly, sabotage check.** A forged testimony acquits when uncaught. With
  the catch roll forced, the verdict derives on the truth and suspicion rises.
- **Budget.** Influence bounds what can be presented. `assign` refuses beyond
  capacity.
- **Carry-over.** Only cast members met in the Season reach the docket. Their
  Season line determines their pool.
- **Persistence.** Mid-Season and mid-session saves survive a fresh-process
  JSON round-trip. Replay is deterministic.
